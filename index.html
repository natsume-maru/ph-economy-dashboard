Step 1: The Basics

What is Gross Domestic Product (GDP)?

GDP is simply the total market value of all final goods and services produced inside a country in a year. Think of it as the nation's report card!

Switch View Analogy

Toggle between a familiar household view and national economic view.

How GDP is Calculated (Expenditure Approach)

GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)

C = Consumption
Filipino families buying groceries, rice, smartphones, dining out, and haircut services.
I = Investments
Businesses buying machines, building offices, buying technology, and construction.
G = Govt Spending
Public roads, bridges, public school teacher salaries, health programs, and defense.
X - M = Net Exports
Goods exported (semiconductors, bananas) minus imported goods (oil, wheat, cars).

Step 5: Everyday Impact

What Does GDP Mean For Your Everyday Life?

Connecting macroeconomic numbers directly to household reality.

Jobs & Opportunities

When GDP grows fast (5%+), businesses hire more workers, open new branches, and upgrade facilities. Lower GDP growth usually means slower job market hiring.

Income & Wages

Higher GDP per capita generally leads to wage growth over time as worker productivity increases and companies compete for skilled workers.

Prices & Purchasing Power

Growth must be balanced with inflation. If GDP grows fast but inflation is high, price increases eat up wage gains. Controlled inflation protects household purchasing power.
